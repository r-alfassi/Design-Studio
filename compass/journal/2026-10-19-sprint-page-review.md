# Review of a long-running sprint page

Observed 2026-10-19 in the Figma file of one of the Designer's personal game projects, by reading its structure. The file holds twenty sprints on one page. This entry is evidence for Q-67 and for backlog entries F-005 and F-006. It governs nothing. Read it when the work touches the sprint layout practice or the open sequence-frame question.

## Practices the file already shows that the skill states

The skill already states these, and the file confirms they work over twenty sprints:

- A sprint direction summary as the first frame of a sprint, kept when later variants are added.
- Rejected branches kept beside the work they were compared with, named with a trailing "R" and numbered in order, 21 frames in the longest sprint.
- A final outcome frame at the far right of a sprint.
- Superseded work kept under a named historical archive frame, and not deleted.

## Evidence for the open items

- **Sequence frame or frame per step (Q-67 task 2).** The first palette tests (sprints 4 to 9) each use one auto-layout sequence frame holding every step. From sprint 10 on, each step is its own frame, with a state in its name. Rejected branches appear only in the per-step sprints. Inference: this supports one frame per step for any test that has a rejected branch, and a test with a single step may stay one frame. The Designer decides. On 2026-10-20 the project unpacked the sequence frames of sprints 4 to 9 and a similar one in sprint 13 into one frame per step. Each step kept its content, gained its own state label, and the sprint section became the direct parent of its steps. The sequence frame had also held the sprint's title and question, which became its direction summary.
- **Sprint group (F-006).** Sprints 5 to 9 are five palette tests of one asset family, each its own section, and two of them sit side by side while the others stack. They are the file's natural candidate for one tinted sprint-group section.
- **Unnamed sections (F-005).** Three unnamed sections hold sprint work, one of them a sprint's final outcome. A fourth is empty, and four more are nested inside one sprint. The final outcome in an unnamed section is the case F-005 already records.

## Practices the file shows that the skill does not state

Open: whether each belongs in the skill.

1. **A trail from a foundation to its sprints.** A foundation frame ends with one evidence line naming the sprints that produced it. A reader of the foundation can then reach the sprint history without a search.
2. **A version in the name of a foundation frame**, such as "v0.2", with the accepted state recorded in the project's change log and not on the canvas.
3. **Two pages for sprints.** The first sprint sits on one page and later sprints on another. The file shows the cost: a reader looking for the sprint history has two places to look. This supports a rule of one sprint page per project.
4. **A supporting experiment placed on the moodboard page** and not inside the sprint it served. The skill already says it belongs inside the sprint, and this is the cost of ignoring that.
5. **The Practice page of the Design Studio Figma file disagrees with the skill.** It says sections are never wrapped inside a larger parent and that one sprint never continues in a neighbouring section. The skill's alternating-axes rule allows a tinted group section, and the project page carries two half-step sprints (14.5 and 16.5) that continue a sprint in a neighbouring section. On 2026-10-20 the page text was updated to match the skill, and the skill now names a half-step sprint, numbered NN.5.
