# Layer 2 — The Rationale

**What it is:** The how and in what order. A slide-by-slide or section-by-section narrative logic document. Describes why each unit exists, what argument it makes, and why it earns the next one. Includes theoretical framework attribution and consolidated source links.

**What it does not do:** Design visuals, write final copy, or make positional commitments not already established in the brief.

**The test:** Can someone read this and reconstruct the argument of the deck without seeing a single slide? Does each unit justify the next?

**Governance:** Owned by the project lead. Can be updated more fluidly than the brief, but any update that implicitly changes a positional commitment must trigger a brief review.

**File naming:** `[ProjectCode]_Rationale.md`

---

## For Research-Based Decks

When the deck presents findings from research — benchmarks, audits, user studies — the Rationale has a structural obligation that non-research decks do not: it must establish an interpretive lens *before* presenting evidence.

Evidence without a lens reads as a feature inventory. The reader accumulates findings without knowing what they add up to or why they matter. The lens tells the reader what question the evidence answers, and why that question is worth asking.

**What the lens typically includes:**
- The user behavior or motivation the research is examining
- The specific question the research is designed to answer
- Why that question is non-trivial — what would be missed or misread without this frame

**The test:** Remove all evidence slides. Do the framing slides that remain still constitute a coherent argument? If yes, the lens is structural. If the framing only makes sense once the evidence is visible, the lens is decorative — it needs to be rebuilt as a standalone argument.
